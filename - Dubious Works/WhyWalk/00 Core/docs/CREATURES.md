# The compiled creature profile in WhyWalk

`scripts/WhyWalk/whywalk_creatures.lua` is generated by
`tools/gen_creatures.py` from `tools/creatures_all.csv` — every `CREA` record
across Morrowind, Tribunal, Bloodmoon, Tamriel Data and OAAB, joined to the
mesh it points at.

```sh
cd "00 Core"
python3 -I tools/gen_creatures.py tools/creatures_all.csv \
    > scripts/WhyWalk/whywalk_creatures.lua
```

917 creatures, 139 distinct shapes, 34 KB. Most creatures are variants of each
other — every kagouti is the same animal with different stats — so the
geometry lives in `SHAPES` and each id holds an index into it.

## What it answers

**Where the rider sits.** `up`, in units above the creature's own origin.
`saddleFromBoundingBox` worked this out at runtime from `getBoundingBox()`,
once per creature, with a log line each time. The box it read is the same one
measured offline here, so the lookup replaces the read.

**Whether it may be ridden at all.** `ride` is false when the record sets the
biped flag or the weapon/shield flag: 161 of the 917, being 112 bipeds and 160
weapon carriers overlapping in 111.

**Whether the measurement is trustworthy.** 48 rows have no usable box — 33
OAAB meshes ship in a BSA the audit could not open, 15 have no explicit
`Bounding Box` node and fall through to an autogenerated AABB. Those carry no
`up` at all, so the runtime falls back for exactly the creatures where falling
back is right.

## The saddle height is an approximation

    up = 0.86 * box_top_z

for a walker. Checked against every hand-measured saddle in
`whywalk_shared.PROFILE`, each of which came from the riding mod's own config:

| creature | measured | box top | up/top |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| guar | 130 | 159.9 | 0.813 |
| kagouti | 120 | 159.9 | 0.751 |
| nix-hound | 110 | 115.6 | 0.951 |
| silt strider | 1223 | 1322.2 | 0.925 |
| BM_frost_boar | 95 | 76.2 | 1.246 |
| netch_bull | 200 | 768.4 | 0.260 |
| skylamp | 90 | 611.0 | 0.147 |

0.86 is the mean of the four ground mounts and fits them within 12%. That is
loose, and honestly so: a kagouti's head rides higher above its back than a
nix-hound's, and no fraction of a bounding box can know that. **PROFILE still
wins wherever it has an entry.**

`BM_frost_boar` is excluded from the fit — its measured saddle is 19 units
*above* its box top, so either the riding mod scales its boar or that entry
was never measured. Either way it cannot constrain a fit.

### This is not an improvement on the old heuristic

`up = centre + 0.72 * halfSize.z` reduces to `0.86 * top` whenever the box
bottom is at zero, which is every ground creature. The two formulas are the
same thing written twice, and the 0.72 that was fitted to one animal happened
to be right. What the table adds is the lookup instead of the read, fliers
handled separately, and somewhere to put a corrected value.

### Fliers: the rider sits on top (changed 2026-11-12)

The rider sits on top of every creature, fliers included. A flier whose box floats more than 20 units off the ground gets `up = 0.98 × box top`: the top of a netch's box is the top of its bell (bull: box top 768, bell measured at 755).

The old rule, `up` = the box bottom, hung the rider under the bell. In game a bull's rider stood among its tentacles, 556 units below the bell.

A flier whose box starts at ground level, such as a cliff racer, uses the walker rule as before.

Since this change, Core measures the back at mount time with rendering rays and prefers that measurement. This table is the fallback when nothing was hit. See *Getting on and off* in the README.

## Bipeds and weapon carriers

Two sources, deliberately:

- the compiled table, for everything in those five plugins;
- `record.isBiped` and `record.canUseWeapons` read live, for everything else.

A riding mod's own mount record is always "everything else", so a mod-added
biped is barred just as firmly without being in the table — and the live
record wins over a table entry that would allow it.

The refusal is reported to the player (`msg_not_a_mount`) rather than silent,
because from their side nothing happening is indistinguishable from a broken
keybind.
