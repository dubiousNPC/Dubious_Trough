# Caravans — the movement model was wrong (2026-10-31b)

A week of tuning the heading did not work because the heading was never the
problem. Your own comparison was the answer:

> IT has a gentle drift between each point, long gradual turns

Immersive Travel does not place its mount on the route at all. From
`main.lua`:

```lua
local d = (nextPos - currentPos):normalized()   -- bearing to the NEXT POINT
local new_facing = math.atan2(d.x, d.y)
... turn toward new_facing, clamped ...
mount.facing = facing
mount.position = currentPos + delta             -- delta along `forward`
```

**It steers, and it moves along its own facing.** Two things follow, and
neither can be reached by tuning:

1. The heading target is the **bearing to a point**, which rotates smoothly
   and continuously as you approach it — not a segment heading, which is
   piecewise constant and jumps at every node. That is the gentle drift.
2. The position is **integrated along the facing**, so velocity and facing are
   the same vector. Sideways motion is not small, it is impossible.

Mine sampled the position from the polyline and rotated the body separately.
Those are two independent quantities, and every disagreement between them *is*
the body moving sideways. I measured it, at 60fps over six real routes:

| model | mean sideways | **worst sideways** | mean deviation | end gap |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| place on path (what you have) | 3.8% | **88.1%** | 0 u | 0 u |
| steer only, no correction | 0.0% | 0.0% | 323 u | 906 u |
| **steer + correction** | **1.0%** | **16.9%** | 64 u | 315 u |
| steer + harder correction | 2.9% | 37.7% | 25 u | 86 u |

**88% sideways** at the sharpest corner — the strider travelling almost
entirely crabwise while pointing somewhere else. That is what you have been
looking at for a week, and unwrapping the angle, aiming further ahead and
changing the turn rate all made it smaller without removing it, because the
model permits it.

### The correction, and what it costs

Pure steering never rejoins the route: it swings wide of every corner, ends
906 units from the stop, and on `seyda neen -> vivec` orbits the destination
forever, because its turning radius (speed ÷ turnRate = 1400 units) is larger
than the distance it needs to close. So the position is pulled gently back
toward where the route says it should be. That pull is the only sideways
component left, and at tau 2.0 it measures 1% of travel on average.

The route is still followed to within 64 units on average, on segments 1500
units long. The strider walks a smoother line than the polyline, which is what
an animal would do.

Turn rate is **1.0 rad/s** (57°/s) rather than 1.4. The faster rates track
more tightly and change direction three times as often to do it, which is the
opposite of long gradual turns; the deviation they buy is invisible.

Arrival is unchanged — still driven by distance travelled, so journey times
are the same — and the dismount uses the route's final point rather than the
strider's, since it can finish a few hundred units short after a wide last
corner and you paid to arrive at the stop.

## The animation: I have stopped guessing and made it report

Three rounds, three inferences, all wrong: that no strider creature existed,
then that cloning one would work (it did animate, and wrecked the ride), then
that Immersive Travel's mesh would animate an activator.

`resolveGait` returns nil when nothing matches and `playGait` returns
**silently** — correct behaviour, since `playBlended` on a missing group is a
no-op, and the reason the question survived three rounds with no evidence.

It now says, once per session:

```
[WhyWalk Caravans] vehicle animation: hasAnimation=false,
  groups runforward=false walkforward=false idle=false, playing nil
```

`animation.hasAnimation` separates the only two possibilities that matter:

- **false** — the object has no animation object at all. No group name helps,
  and no fallback chain will. Whether an OpenMW *activator* gets one from a
  mesh with a paired `.kf` is exactly what nobody here has established, and
  this answers it in one line.
- **true** — it can animate and the names are simply wrong, which the group
  list then settles.

### What I can tell you now, with certainty

I checked: **OpenMW's Lua API exposes no way to disable collision.** The only
collision call is `debug.toggleCollision`, a global debug switch. So:

- a **creature** animates and is an actor, whose collision put you inside it,
  locked the camera and killed you;
- an **activator** is safe and may well be unable to animate at all.

If the diagnostic says `hasAnimation=false`, those are not two options with a
bug between them — they are the whole option space, and an animated strider
you can safely ride is not currently reachable this way. Ben's Rideable Silt
Striders reaches the same conclusion: activator strider, separate small
collider for the player, no leg movement.

## Boats: instrumented, not guessed

Boarding threw on an unprefixed model path until yesterday, so the helm code
**had never run in a game**. "Finally registering, but no movement" is the
first report from code nothing had exercised, and I am not going to guess
which link is broken when it can say:

```
[WhyWalk Boats] helm after 1s: throttle=0.00 mv=0.00 side=0.00
  run=false speed=0.0 locked=true gain=1.00
```

That separates the four things that each produce no movement: the throttle
never arriving, the physics returning nothing, the movement override not being
taken, or the engine refusing the controls while `mv` is non-zero.

**The lag when looking at a boat** I could not reproduce from the log — the
hull-measurement line appears once, and nothing in Boats runs per frame
outside a voyage. `lookedAt()` goes through `I.SharedRay`, which is the one
thing that *does* work per frame while you look around; that is where I would
look next, and the helm line will tell us whether a voyage is even active when
it happens.

## Verification

`test_caravans` **123**, `test_intercept` 91, `test_saddlebox` 51,
`test_boats` 213, `test_boats_scripts` 64, `test_resolveanim` 67,
`test_mountcontrols` 34, `test_ridinganim`. luacheck, check_load,
check_handlers, check_names, globalcheck, check_declorder, check_enums,
ctxcheck, check_manifest clean on all three modules.

The steering tests measure the **property**, not the constants. They ride six
real routes and assert:

- the old model really did travel mostly sideways at its worst (>60%), so the
  comparison is against something real;
- steering cuts the worst case at least threefold;
- typical sideways motion stays under 2% of travel;
- every route still completes, mean deviation under 150 units, worst swing
  under 700, and the strider finishes within 500 units of the stop;
- **the correction earns its place**: removing it drops sideways motion to
  zero *and* leaves the strider far from the stop and wandering three times
  further from the route, while a harder pull measurably puts the fault back.

That last pair is the one worth having. Without it, somebody could delete the
correction and the sideways assertions would pass perfectly while the strider
orbited Vivec.

## What to look for

1. **The turning.** This is a different movement model, not a tuned one. The
   strider should drift between points and never crab.
2. **`vehicle animation:` in the log.** One line, and it settles three rounds
   of guessing either way.
3. **`helm after 1s:` in the log**, after boarding a boat and holding forward.
4. Whether the lag on looking at a boat happens while piloting or not.
