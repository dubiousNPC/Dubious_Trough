## 1. The strider's animation: there is no vanilla silt strider creature

That was the assumption behind both previous attempts, and the profile you
sent settles it. Of 224 Morrowind `CREA` records, **not one is a strider**.
The ones you ride into town are statics.

The only strider creatures in the wild come from Tamriel Data and OAAB, and
they do not use the vanilla mesh either:

| record | plugin | model |
|---|---|---|
| `T_Mw_Fau_Slstrid_01` | Tamriel Data | `TR\cr\TR_siltstrider_VO.NIF` |
| `AB_Fau_Siltstrider01` | OAAB | `OAAB\r\tr_siltstrider_vo.nif` |

So matching the basename `siltstrider.nif` found nothing even after the sweep
was fixed — the file is called `tr_siltstrider_vo.nif`. Both ids are now tried
directly, and the model test matches a **token** in the basename rather than
the whole filename, since the three names share only the word.

One consequence to expect: those two carry `idle` and `walkforward` but **no
`runforward`**, so the gait falls back to `walkforward` — the profile notes
the strider "runs and swims on the walk cycle" anyway. And the vehicle will
be the TR/OAAB strider model rather than the vanilla one. It is a silt
strider; it is not pixel-identical to the static beside it.

## 3. Orientation: the body was chasing the path and never catching it

This one I could measure. `easeHeading` is a first-order ease, and a
first-order ease **always trails a moving target** — the polyline's heading
jumps at every waypoint, so the target never stops moving. Simulated at 60fps
over eight real routes with the shipped constants:

> mean **5.2 degrees** off its direction of travel, worst **74.7**, and more
> than 10 degrees off for **18.7%** of every ride.

A strider visibly walking sideways through every bend. Raising the turn rate
treats the symptom; the cause is that it starts turning *after* the corner.

**Lookahead** fixes the cause: aim at the path 350 units further on — a
quarter of a second at 1400 u/s — so the turn begins before the corner, the
way anything that steers is actually driven.

| turnRate | lookahead | mean | worst | >10° |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.9 | 0 | 5.2 | 74.7 | 18.7% | ← what shipped |
| 0.9 | 350 | 3.3 | 61.8 | 12.0% |
| **1.4** | **350** | **2.3** | **51.7** | **9.9%** | ← chosen |
| 2.0 | 350 | 2.2 | 42.5 | 9.5% |
| 1.4 | 1400 | 12.0 | 80.2 | 42.8% | ← too much anticipation |

1.4 rather than 2.0 because 115 degrees a second is a hovercraft, not a very
large slow animal. Past about 700 units of lookahead every measure gets worse
again — the body turns for a corner it has not reached and is wrong on the
straight instead.

The residual worst case is a near-180-degree waypoint, which no finite turn
rate can follow instantly and which a real animal would also take time over.

Costs one extra `path.at` per frame: a binary search over a few dozen points.

**If "imperfect" meant something other than lag** — facing backwards, leaning,
or crabbing on the straights rather than in bends — say so, because this fixes
lag specifically.

## Core: the compiled creature profile

`scripts/WhyWalk/whywalk_creatures.lua`, generated by
`tools/gen_creatures.py` from the CSV you sent. 917 creatures, 139 distinct
shapes, 34 KB — most creatures are variants of each other, so the geometry
lives in `SHAPES` and each id holds an index. `00 Core/CREATURES.md` has the
full account; the short version:

**Ride height.** `up = 0.86 * box_top_z` for a walker, the box bottom for a
genuine flier. Honest caveat: this is **not** an improvement on the old
runtime heuristic. `centre + 0.72 * halfSize.z` reduces to `0.86 * top`
whenever the box bottom is zero, which is every ground creature — the two are
the same formula and the 0.72 fitted to one animal happened to be right. What
the table adds is a lookup instead of a per-mount `getBoundingBox()` read,
fliers handled separately, and somewhere to put a corrected value.

Checked against all nine hand-measured saddles; it lands within 12% of the
four ground mounts, and **PROFILE still wins wherever it has an entry**.
`BM_frost_boar` is excluded from the fit because its measured saddle is 19
units *above* its own box top.

**48 rows have no usable box** — 33 OAAB meshes in an unopened BSA, 15 with no
explicit `Bounding Box`. They carry no `up`, so the runtime falls back for
exactly the creatures where falling back is right.

## Core: bipeds and weapon carriers cannot be ridden

161 of the 917 are barred — 112 bipeds and 160 weapon carriers, overlapping in
111.

Two sources, deliberately. The table answers for those five plugins; for
anything else the live record carries `isBiped` and `canUseWeapons`, so a
**mod-added** biped is barred just as firmly without being in the table. The
live record wins over a table entry that would allow it — asserted, since a
riding mod's own mount record is always "anything else".

Refused with a message rather than silently: from the player's side nothing
happening is indistinguishable from a broken keybind.
