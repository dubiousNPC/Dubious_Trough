# Caravans — reverting the steering, and what the log settled (2026-11-03)

## The collision regression is mine, and it is reverted

The steering model integrated the strider's position along its own facing
instead of sampling it from the route. That removed the sideways travel and
put the strider through the scenery: it swung up to 500 units wide of corners,
and the route is drawn through streets, so wide means inside a building.

"Begins in the wrong position, huge collision issues, getting stuck in
geometry" is that change. The line is traced exactly again.

I should have seen it: the route was authored to be *traced*. Any model that
departs from it by hundreds of units will hit what it was drawn to avoid.

---

## The trilemma, which is why this has taken a week

I measured all four models over six real routes at 60fps. A vehicle following
a hand-drawn polyline cannot have all three of these:

**(a)** the exact authored line · **(b)** gentle turns · **(c)** pointing where
it moves

| model | mean sideways | 99th pct turn | deviation |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| trace, facing filtered toward a heading | 3.8% | 80°/s | 0 u |
| trace, facing exactly along the step | **0.0%** | **4144°/s** | 0 u |
| steer with correction | 1.0% | 57°/s | **494 u** |
| trace, facing = bearing ahead (now) | 3.3% | 207°/s | 0 u |

Row 1 is (a)+(b) — a week of crabbing. Row 2 is (a)+(c) — no crabbing, snaps
round at every node. Row 3 is (b)+(c) — Immersive Travel's model, and the
collisions you just had.

**The reason is the data.** I measured all 2056 node turns in the shipped
routes: the median is **22.4 degrees** and a third turn more than 30. At 1400
units a second a node arrives about once a second. So pointing exactly along
the current step means a 22-degree snap every second, and pointing anywhere
smoother means pointing *across* the corner you are approaching — by roughly
the corner's own angle.

That is not a tuning failure. No constant fixes it, which is why none of mine
did.

### What I have shipped, and the lever you now have

The line is traced exactly. The facing is the **bearing to a point further
along it** — and critically, it has **no state**: a pure function of distance
travelled. It cannot lag, accumulate, wrap, drift, or spin the long way, which
between them account for every facing bug this module has had. There is
nothing to converge and nothing to go wrong.

The carrot distance is now a setting, **Turn smoothness**, default 350:

| ahead | mean sideways | 99th pct turn |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 150 | 1.5% | 520°/s |
| 250 | 2.6% | 266°/s |
| **350** | **3.3%** | **207°/s** |
| 700 | 6.9% | 121°/s |
| 1200 | 11.5% | 77°/s |

350 puts the crabbing no worse than the filtered model that shipped for a
week, with no filter to lag. Slide it down for tight and snappy, up for gentle
and leaning. **There is no value that is both**, and I have stopped choosing
for you — picking it myself went badly in both directions.

**The real fix is the route data.** One pass of Chaikin corner-cutting halves
the median node turn to 10.7 degrees, which would buy gentleness and
square-on travel together. That belongs in the importer with the deviation
measured properly, not in a constant here, and I have not shipped it
unmeasured.

## The animation: settled, by the game

```
[WhyWalk Caravans] vehicle animation: hasAnimation=true,
  groups runforward=false walkforward=false idle=true, playing idle
```

Three rounds of inference, answered in one line:

- **The activator does have an animation object.** `hasAnimation=true`.
- **It has `idle` and nothing else.** That is the vanilla strider mesh's own
  baked-in idle — the reason the striders standing in towns visibly breathe.
- **`runforward` and `walkforward` are absent.** Those live in
  `xsiltstrider.kf`, and OpenMW loads a paired `.kf` for **actors** only.

So the strider *is* animating — it is playing `idle`, which the log confirms.
The walk cycle needs a creature, a creature is an actor, and an actor's
collision is what put you inside it, locked the camera and killed you on
30 October. OpenMW's Lua API has no way to disable collision; I checked, and
the only call is `debug.toggleCollision`, a global debug switch.

**That is the whole option space.** An idling activator or a walking actor you
cannot safely ride. Ben's Rideable Silt Striders reaches the same answer.

## Your MWScript pin is not running

```
[WhyWalk] The MWScript rider pin is not running; its globals exist but nothing
consumes them. ... recompile WhyWalkRiderPin in the OpenMW-CS script editor
(open it and save) to fix this properly. Falling back to the Lua teleport pin.
```

This also corrects me: on 30 October I inferred from a *missing* log line that
the pin was live. It is not. My diagnostic reports at 60 frames, before Core's
liveness check has returned its verdict at 20 misses, so it printed the
optimistic answer — the same mistake in a different place.

Open `WhyWalkRiderPin` in OpenMW-CS and save it. Until then the player is
teleported every frame, which is the `onTeleported` storm and part of why
being stuck in geometry is hard to get out of.

## "Teleporting is blocked": I found no mechanism in WhyWalk

I searched the whole suite. Nothing calls `setTeleportingEnabled`, nothing
touches a control switch, and the only persistent actor state applied anywhere
is a Levitate effect in Core's **mount** path and water walking in **Boats** —
neither on a caravan journey, and both removed on their own paths.

My working explanation is the collision: with the strider inside geometry and
the player pinned to it by the teleport fallback, the player is inside a solid,
and the engine refuses travel and intervention from there. Tracing the line
again should clear it.

If it persists after this build, I need the specific refusal — a message on
screen, which service, and whether an ordinary door transition also fails.
Without that I would be guessing again, and I have done enough of that.

## Verification

`test_caravans` **123**, `test_intercept` 91, `test_saddlebox` 51,
`test_boats` 213, `test_boats_scripts` 64, `test_resolveanim` 67,
`test_mountcontrols` 34, `test_ridinganim`. All checkers clean on all three
modules.

The assertion that would have caught this regression:

```
check('the body stays exactly on the authored route',
      now.worstDev < 1e-6)
```

Deviation from the authored line is now a tested property, not an incidental
one. The steering build measured 494 units against that bar.

Two more worth naming, both written so nobody repeats the last week:

- **the worst sideways case is asserted to be the data**, not the model — the
  median node turn must exceed 15 degrees and the worst sideways must be of
  that order, so the next person reads that it cannot be tuned out;
- **the facing is asserted to be a pure function** of distance travelled,
  called before and after fifty other queries and required to return the
  identical value.

## What to look for

1. **Collisions and getting stuck** — the line is exact again, so this is the
   one I expect to be gone.
2. **Whether travel still refuses** once you are not stuck, and if so exactly
   how it refuses.
3. **Turn smoothness** in the Caravans settings, if 350 is not where you want
   to sit on that trade.
4. The strider idling rather than walking is now the known ceiling, not a bug
   — say if you would rather I pursued smoother route data, which is the only
   thing that would change the trade.
